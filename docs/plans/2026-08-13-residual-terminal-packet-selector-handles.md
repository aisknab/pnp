# Residual terminal Packet selector-handle plan

## Legacy anchor

Pinned manuscript Section 11's Packet edge requires finite selectors to be
encoded, faithful to their selected support, and compatible with the later
realizer and route interfaces. The preceding reconstructed milestone proves
only that every payload-backed Packet seed belongs to the exact
grouped-footprint list supplied by an explicit BN6 family.

## Unbounded abstraction

Work over every arbitrary finite explicit grouped BN6 family and arbitrary
atom and payload types. A handle is a finite position in that family's exact
grouped-footprint list. Its bound must be derived from the input list, never
from a fixed carrier size or enumerated example.

## Exact theorem boundary

Define canonical finite handles and their total footprint decoder. Prove that
decoding is injective because grouped footprints are duplicate-free. Prove
that every decoded footprint remains inside the family's carrier, has length
at least two, and retains an original grouped cell and atom payload witness.
Prove that a footprint is a payload selector exactly when a handle decodes to
it, and strengthen this to existence of a unique handle. Upgrade every pair,
positive balanced-triple, and full-span Packet branch without changing its
logical alternatives.

## Remaining downstream blockers

These handles are input-relative list positions, not the manuscript's bit
encoding or a polynomially enumerable selector universe. The milestone does
not prove manuscript-level selector faithfulness or compatibility, construct
a selector realizer or route, derive or group BN6 survivors from a terminal
candidate, establish polynomial encoding length or runtime, complete PkgC,
ZeroSlack, or PCCMin, put CNF-SAT in P, remove a project assumption, or prove
`P = NP`.

## Required evidence

- Lean theorem module over arbitrary finite grouped families.
- Axiom audit with an exact public declaration list.
- Generic regression covering decoding, uniqueness, retained evidence, and
  every Packet branch.
- Hostile JavaScript audit rejecting fixed bounds, non-injective decoding,
  detached payloads, weakened branch coverage, and overclaims.
- Synchronized theorem inventory, publication map, status payloads, report,
  documentation, and durable read-only workflow checks.
