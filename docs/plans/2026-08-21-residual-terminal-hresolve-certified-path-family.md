# M176 terminal HResolve certified-path family

## Evidence-led selection

The current reconstruction separately proves two bounded Section 8 kernels.
`ResidualTerminalHResolveHDisjointFamily` assembles a maximal H-disjoint
family over supplied eight-domain footprints, while
`ResidualTerminalHNBWLCertifiedPathMinimum` selects an exact four-coordinate
minimum from one supplied complete certified-path family. The remaining
bounded composition gap is that selected HResolve candidates do not yet carry
and expose those path-minimum certificates.

M176 closes that interface without claiming the missing terminal derivation.
Each supplied hereditary candidate owns its expected frontier, footprint,
nonempty finite certified-path family, governed predicate, completeness proof,
and path-to-footprint coherence proof.

## Target

For every duplicate-free finite family of supplied proof-bearing hereditary
candidates:

1. reflect candidate H-disjointness through the existing exact eight-domain
   footprint checker;
2. compute each candidate's exact four-coordinate certified-path minimum;
3. preserve semantic, frontier, block, shape, and footprint coherence for that
   minimum;
4. greedily construct a duplicate-free pairwise H-disjoint selected family;
5. prove every selected candidate came from the supplied family;
6. prove every rejected supplied candidate has a selected blocker with its
   exact first interference route; and
7. expose all of that evidence through one named bounded endpoint.

The endpoint is
`PNP.DirectWire.terminal_hresolve_certified_path_family_complete`.

## Claim boundary

The candidates, paths, footprints, governed predicates, completeness proofs,
and path-to-footprint coherence proofs remain supplied. M176 does not derive
them from terminal data; prove HN grammar soundness or completeness, LN
confluence, ParseOrExit, independent leaf tightness, or the H0-H4 sidecar; or
connect blockers to HB rank semantics. It is not full or polynomial HResolve,
the complete no-lower ledger, unconditional ZeroSlack, PCCMin, polynomial
runtime, concrete SAT in P, the root theorem, or project-axiom removal.
